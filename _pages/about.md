I am currently a Ph.D. candidate in [Imaging Science](https://engineering.wustl.edu/academics/programs/imaging-science/index.html) at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. [Matthew D. Lew](https://lewlab.wustl.edu/). My research focuses on multi-dimensional single-molecule nanoscopy, specifically single-molecule orientation-localization microscopy (SMOLM). I am interested in developing optical and computational tools for simultaneously measuring single-molecule positions and orientations, thereby resolving the local chemical environments and conformational dynamics of biomolecules at the nanoscale.

Prior to WashU, I received my B.S. in Physics from Nankai University as a member of the Poling Class of Physics. I studied the deformability and membrane organization of human red blood cells using microfluidics and single-molecule localization microscopy under Prof. [Leiting Pan](http://panltlab.org/). In the summer of 2019, I was a visiting student in Prof. [Qiong Yang](https://websites.umich.edu/~qiongy/)'s Lab in the Department of Biophysics at University of Michigan.

More details can be found in my [CV](/files/cv_YiyangCHEN.pdf).
